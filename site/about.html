THE SHORT VERSION

Ground change.
Human context.

groundstory uses NISAR orbital radar to understand human stories on the ground. Repeated passes provide phase, coherence and radar brightness. We use NASA/JPL’s published GUNW and GCOV products—not raw echoes we processed ourselves.

Find → clean and map → ground stories → visualize. We check coverage, stream local data windows, correct and mask the measurements, compare a quiet baseline, align terrain and Sentinel-2, locate sourced accounts and review them. Optical imagery, stories, radar interpretation and 3D terrain become views of the same place.

The schoolyard photograph, shrine and junction are GSJ/AIST field observations in Kumamoto. The school’s 94 cm sideways offset and shrine’s 95 cm offset are independent ground-survey figures—not NISAR pixel values. The radar supplies the wider line-of-sight movement pattern.

One repeatable pipeline, twelve built events, six event types. The film shows twelve genuinely rotating terrain meshes using their own Copernicus DEM and radar/optical layers. Different event types need different signals: phase for motion, coherence loss for surface change, and backscatter for fires, floods and shoreline change. This demonstrates reuse across the existing catalogue, not unlimited throughput or automatic success on any event.

Findings: located accounts can be connected to measured ground differences; whether this improves understanding remains an untested hypothesis. At Etna the mapped lava-associated change is about 2.5 km², missing the already-incoherent lower flow. The new coastal land is at Anak Krakatau, not Etna: dual-look, multi-pass backscatter estimates 26.5 ha of shoreline gain, with an optical cross-check. The reported official estimate is 32.8 ha and provisional; no published comparison polygon is available. The area ratio is not a spatial-accuracy score.

Future work: test radar-derived sideways motion. The split-screen animation is the current survey + GPS model, exaggerated, not a radar-derived result. One radar viewing direction does not recover the full motion vector. In-project techniques include dual-look shoreline differencing and sampling radar at located accounts; we are not claiming global methodological novelty.

Sources, scientific limits & recording

Field photographs: GSJ / AIST Emergency field report No. 5, photos 10–12. Akai, Ota, Maruyama, Yoshimi, Azuma and Ninomiya / AIST Geological Survey of Japan. School (photo 10); Yatsushiro Junction (photo 11); shrine (photo 12). Presentation crop/reframing and English captions by Dancing With The SARs; original photograph bytes and source annotations unchanged. GSJ does not endorse this presentation. Reuse terms (CC BY 4.0 compatible).

Radar: NISAR L2 GUNW and GCOV (provisional), NASA/JPL-Caltech/ISRO, distributed by ASF DAAC. Phase, coherence and backscatter are published products; our work corrects, masks, maps and connects them to ground accounts. Kumamoto colours show toward/away motion, one cycle per 10 cm—not a native phase cycle. Blank/no-data areas are not measurements. Terrain height is never LOS uplift.

Ground imagery: Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel-2 data (ESA). Terrain: © DLR e.V. 2010–2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014–2018, provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA. Terrain relief is exaggerated for legibility; event tiles have independent spatial scales and are not area comparisons.

Findings: the Etna event states the partial footprint and missed lower flow; the Anak Krakatau event gives the dual-look method, strict-threshold sensitivity and comparison with the reported 32.8 ha. Neither is a complete independent validation.

Slip: GSJ tape measurements at nine sites; Nevada Geodetic Laboratory positions for GSI GEONET stations. The interpolated model is imperfect and omits vertical movement. The white arrow is the measured GPS step, at the same exaggeration; not the model prediction. Grid: 5 km. Explore the prototype and limitations.

Create: a documented developer-operated workflow, conditional on coverage and an interpretable signal. Not an all-in-one creation app. It requires source-repository access, Earthdata credentials, technical setup and human review; a fresh end-to-end new-event build has not yet been demonstrated.
