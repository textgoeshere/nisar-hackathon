THE SHORT VERSION

Ground change.
Human context.

What does ground change mean for everyday places? We put NISAR’s measured ground change alongside sourced accounts from the places affected.

At Ryuho Elementary School in Kumamoto, a Geological Survey of Japan field survey measured a 94 cm sideways offset across the schoolyard. That is an independent ground measurement—not a NISAR pixel value. The radar supplies the wider, line-of-sight movement pattern.

The sideways-slip prototype makes a limitation tangible: one radar viewing direction cannot recover the full motion vector. Its animation is a survey + GPS model, exaggerated, not sideways motion measured by NISAR. The future-work question is whether radar offsets can recover that component.

We have connected the measurements and accounts. Whether that improves understanding remains a hypothesis, not a demonstrated user-study result.

Photo: GSJ / AIST field report, photo 10. Akai, Ota, Maruyama, Yoshimi, Azuma and Ninomiya / AIST Geological Survey of Japan. Presentation crop/reframing and English caption by Dancing With The SARs; original photograph bytes and source arrows unchanged. Reuse terms.

Radar: NISAR L2 GUNW (provisional), NASA/JPL-Caltech/ISRO, distributed by ASF DAAC. Phase and coherence are published products; our work corrects, masks, maps and connects them to ground accounts. Colours show toward/away motion; height in this presentation is terrain only. The colour scale is one cycle per 10 cm, not a native radar phase cycle. Blank/no-data areas are not measurements.

Ground imagery: Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel-2 data (ESA). Terrain: © DLR e.V. 2010–2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014–2018, provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA.

Slip: GSJ tape measurements at nine sites; Nevada Geodetic Laboratory positions for GSI GEONET stations. The interpolated model is imperfect and omits vertical movement. The white arrow is the measured GPS step, shown at the same exaggeration; it is not the model prediction. Explore the prototype and its limitations.
